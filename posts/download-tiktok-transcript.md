TikTok gives you no way to save the words in a video. Not as a file, not as a copy-paste, not as anything. The captions it shows on screen are drawn onto playback and cannot be selected.

So "downloading a TikTok transcript" means generating the text first, then exporting it. Here is how, and which of the three formats you actually want.

## How to download a TikTok transcript

**Step 1: Copy the link.** Tap the arrow on the right of the video → **Copy link**. On desktop, copy the URL.

**Step 3: Click Get Transcript.** The full spoken text appears in a few seconds.

## Which format should you download?

The three options are not interchangeable — each is right for a different job.

### TXT — plain text

The default, and the right answer most of the time. No formatting, no metadata, just the words. Use it when you want to:

- Paste into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini
- Drop into Notion, Obsidian or Apple Notes
- Keep an archive that will still open in twenty years
- Feed into a script or spreadsheet

If you are unsure, choose TXT.

### DOCX — Word document

Use this when a human is going to edit the text. DOCX opens in Word, Google Docs and Pages, and supports highlighting, comments and tracked changes. Right for:

- Turning a script into a draft blog post
- Sharing with a client or teammate for markup
- Anything that ends up in a document rather than a prompt

### SRT — subtitle file

A timed subtitle file, with timestamps mapping each line to a moment in the video. Only useful if something is going to *play* the video alongside it. Right for:

- Reposting a clip to YouTube, Instagram or LinkedIn with proper captions
- Loading into a video editor
- Accessibility compliance where a real caption track is required

SRT is the wrong choice for pasting into an AI tool — the timestamps are noise that eats into the context and adds nothing.

| Format | Best for | Opens in |
|---|---|---|
| TXT | AI tools, notes, archives | Anything |
| DOCX | Editing, sharing, drafting | Word, Google Docs, Pages |
| SRT | Subtitles, video editors | Editors, YouTube, players |

## Downloading on mobile

The whole flow works in a phone browser, which matters because most people find the TikTok they want on their phone in the first place.

1. In the TikTok app, tap **Share** → **Copy link**
2. Open your browser and go to transcribetok.com
3. Paste, generate, tap **Download**

On iPhone the file saves to the **Files** app (Downloads by default). On Android it goes to your **Downloads** folder. Nothing to install either way.

## Common problems

**"The transcript is mostly empty."** The video probably has little or no speech — a dance, a montage, or a clip where the message is entirely on-screen text. Transcription works from audio; text overlays are graphics and cannot be captured.

**"Some words are wrong."** Names, brands and slang are the usual suspects, especially with loud music underneath. Fix them on a read-through before you export — it takes under a minute for a short clip.

**"The link won't work."** Make sure the video is public. Private accounts, deleted videos and region-blocked content cannot be accessed. Both the long URL and the short `vm.tiktok.com` share link are fine.
