## What a TikTok transcript generator does

A transcript generator takes the audio track from a TikTok video and runs it through speech recognition, returning the result as plain text.

That is worth distinguishing from two things it is often confused with:

- **TikTok's on-screen captions** are burned into the viewing experience. They exist to be read while watching, not extracted. Even when a creator enables them, there is no way to select or export the text from the app.
- **Creator-added text overlays** are graphics, not text data. They are pixels on a video frame.

A transcript generator bypasses both by working from the audio itself. That is why it still returns a full transcript on videos where the creator never touched the captions setting.

## Why not just read TikTok's captions?

Because reading is not the same as having. TikTok's auto-captions are genuinely good — they support many languages and cost nothing — but they are a display feature, not a data feature. Everything you would realistically want to *do* with a script requires the text:

| What you want to do | Captions on screen | Extracted transcript |
|---|---|---|
| Read along while watching | Yes | Yes |
| Search for a specific phrase | No | Yes |
| Paste into ChatGPT or Claude | No | Yes |
| Save the script for later | No | Yes |
| Translate the whole video | Partially | Yes |
| Compare 50 videos side by side | No | Yes |

The gap widens fast once you move past a single video. Watching 50 TikToks to find every hook a competitor uses takes an afternoon. Reading 50 transcripts takes ten minutes, and you can Ctrl-F them.

## Accuracy: what to expect honestly

Automatic speech recognition on TikTok content is harder than on, say, a podcast. Three things fight it:

1. **Music.** A loud trending sound under the voice measurably reduces accuracy.
2. **Speed.** TikTok delivery is fast, often deliberately sped up in editing.
3. **Slang and code-switching.** Platform-native slang, brand names, and mid-sentence switches between languages are the most common errors.

For clear speech in a well-supported language, expect accuracy in the mid-to-high 90s — good enough to search, summarize, and quote from after a quick read-through. For anything you plan to publish as a direct quote, read the line back against the video first. That is true of every automatic transcription tool, not just this one.
