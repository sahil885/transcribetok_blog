TikTok has two separate subtitle systems, they behave differently, and neither of them will give you a file. That last part is what sends most people looking for a TikTok subtitle generator in the first place — you can get subtitles onto a TikTok easily enough, but getting them back out, or bringing an existing subtitle file in, is where the platform stops helping.

This guide covers both built-in systems as TikTok documents them, then the cases where you genuinely need an external tool.

## First, a naming problem worth clearing up

"Caption" means two unrelated things on TikTok, and searching for a **TikTok caption generator** returns tools for both.

- **The post caption** is the text under the video, with the hashtags. It is written by hand. TikTok has no automatic generator for it.
- **Subtitles**, sometimes called closed captions, are the timed on-screen text of what is being said. This is what TikTok's caption tools actually produce.

If words on the video is what you want, you want subtitles. Everything below is about subtitles.

## TikTok's two built-in subtitle systems

TikTok documents these separately in its help centre, and the difference matters.

**Auto-generated captions** use speech recognition and are produced automatically for eligible videos you upload. They exist for accessibility, so viewers can switch them off from the share panel. You control them through the language setting: on the post screen, tap **More options → Select video language**, then pick the spoken language. You can edit them after posting — tap the caption on your live video, tap **Edit captions**, change or remove any line, then tap **Save**. Editing captions on a video that is already published is unusual on TikTok and worth knowing about.

**Creator captions** are the styled ones. Tap **Add post (+)**, record or upload, continue to the preview screen, then tap **Captions** on the side panel. TikTok transcribes the speech automatically. The first time you use it you are prompted to opt in. You then preview and edit each line before saving, and you can change font style and colour. Creator captions are part of the video content rather than an accessibility overlay.

Practical rule: **use creator captions when the look matters, auto-generated captions when accessibility matters.** Both are transcription, so both need the same manual pass over names, numbers and brand terms.

## What TikTok will not do

**TikTok does not accept subtitle file uploads.** There is no SRT, VTT or SBV import anywhere in the consumer posting flow. If you already have a subtitle file — from an editor, a translator, or a previous version of the video — the only ways to get it onto a TikTok are to burn it into the video before uploading, or to retype it into TikTok's own caption tools.

**TikTok does not export captions either.** There is no copy, download or export control for captions anywhere in the app or on the web, on your own videos or anyone else's. This is the constraint behind almost every question in this category, and we cover the workaround in [how to download TikTok captions and subtitles](/download-tiktok-captions).

**Auto-captions do not cover everything.** Coverage runs roughly 60–75% of videos and is worse for non-English audio, for videos posted before late 2023, in the first 24–48 hours after upload, and for slideshows, dance and music-led clips with little speech. A video with no captions after 48 hours usually never gets them.

**One exception, on the business side.** TikTok Ads Manager's Video Editor does generate captions and can translate them into other languages, with font, colour and alignment controls. It is a separate surface from the consumer app and applies to advertising creative, not to ordinary posts.

## When you need an external subtitle generator

Four situations where the built-in tools run out:

1. **You need a file.** Any workflow that leaves TikTok — repurposing to YouTube or Reels, an editor, a subtitle translator, an archive — needs an SRT or VTT. TikTok produces neither.
2. **You are subtitling someone else's video.** Competitive research, accessibility audits, education. You cannot use TikTok's creator tools on a video you did not post.
3. **You want styling TikTok does not offer.** Animated word-by-word subtitles, brand fonts, karaoke highlighting. That is an editor's job, and an editor wants an SRT.
4. **The audio is hard.** Heavy music, sped-up delivery, code-switching. A dedicated transcription tool with a full editing view is easier to correct than TikTok's line-by-line panel.

## Getting a usable subtitle file

The route that works for any public TikTok, including videos you did not post:

1. Copy the video link from the share sheet.
2. Paste it into a transcript tool that transcribes the audio track rather than scraping TikTok's caption data — that distinction is what lets it work on videos with no captions at all.
3. Export as **SRT**. That is the subtitle format every editor reads.
4. Correct names, numbers and brand terms. Do this once, in the file, before it reaches an editor.
5. Load the SRT into your editor and burn it in, or upload it to a platform that accepts subtitle files.

## Format notes that save an hour

| | SRT | VTT |
|---|---|---|
| Header line | None | `WEBVTT` plus a blank line, required |
| Milliseconds separator | Comma — `00:00:01,500` | Period — `00:00:01.500` |
| Cue numbers | Required | Optional |
| Styling blocks | None | `STYLE`, `REGION`, `NOTE` |
| Used by | Most video editors, most social platforms | HTML5 `<track>`, browsers |
| TikTok accepts it | No | No |

Renaming an `.srt` file to `.vtt` does not convert it — the header is missing and the timecodes are still comma-separated, so browsers reject it silently. The full conversion is a ten-second manual edit, covered in the [SRT to VTT converter guide](/srt-to-vtt-converter).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does TikTok have a subtitle generator?**
Yes, two of them. Auto-generated captions are an accessibility layer that TikTok produces automatically for eligible uploads and that viewers can switch off. Creator captions are transcribed in the posting flow, can be styled with different fonts and colours, and become part of the video. Neither one lets you export a subtitle file.

**Can I upload an SRT file to TikTok?**
No. TikTok's consumer app has no subtitle-file upload anywhere in the posting flow. If you have an SRT you want on a TikTok, you have to burn it into the video in an editor before uploading, or retype it using TikTok's own caption tools.

**How do I edit TikTok's auto-generated captions after posting?**
Open your posted video, tap the caption on the video, then tap Edit captions. You can change or delete any line, then tap Save at the top. This works after the video is live, which is not true of most in-app editing on TikTok.

**What is the difference between auto-generated captions and creator captions on TikTok?**
Auto-generated captions are an accessibility feature: TikTok creates them from your selected video language, viewers can turn them off from the share panel, and they cannot be styled. Creator captions are opted into during creation, are editable line by line, support font and colour changes, and are part of the video content.

**Is a TikTok caption generator the same as a TikTok subtitle generator?**
Usually not. A caption generator normally writes the post text and hashtags that sit under the video. A subtitle generator produces the timed on-screen text of what is being said. If you want words on the video, you want subtitles; if you want words under the video, you want the post caption, which TikTok has no automatic tool for.
