You found a TikTok that's clearly useful — a recipe, a tutorial, a product breakdown — and it's in a language you don't read. You tap around looking for a translate option, find one, tap it, and it translates the caption underneath the video. Not a word of what the creator is actually saying.

That gap is the whole problem, and it's a deliberate one.

## What TikTok's translation actually covers

TikTok's **See Translation** button applies to the written text attached to a post: the caption, the description, and text stickers. It appears when TikTok detects that text is in a different language from your device setting, and it's free. There's an auto-translation setting you can turn on so it happens without tapping.

None of that touches the audio. The spoken words — which on a tutorial or explainer video are most of the actual content — are untranslated and untouched by the feature.

And even for the text it does translate, there's no way to get it out. TikTok provides no copy, export or download anywhere in the app or on the web. You can read the translation on screen and that's the end of it.

So translating what's *said* in a TikTok is always two steps: get the words into text, then translate the text.

## The two-step method

**Step 1: Get the transcript in the original language.**

**Step 2: Translate the text.**

Copy the **TXT** output, not the SRT. Timecodes confuse most translation tools and you'll spend longer repairing the file than translating it. Then paste into whichever of these fits:

- **DeepL** — the strongest for European languages, and noticeably more natural than the alternatives on long paragraphs.
- **Google Translate** — the widest language coverage, and fine for gist.
- **ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity** — the best option for TikTok specifically, for reasons below.

## Why AI tools usually beat literal translators here

Short-form video is a hostile environment for literal translation. It's compressed, it leans on slang, it's frequently ironic, and it uses platform-native phrasing that has no dictionary equivalent. A word-for-word translator renders all of that flatly and sometimes backwards.

The fix is giving a model the context a translator can't accept:

> Below is the transcript of a TikTok in Brazilian Portuguese by a skincare creator, aimed at a general audience. Translate it into natural English. Keep the informal register — don't make it sound like a press release. Where a phrase is slang or an idiom with no direct equivalent, translate the meaning and add the literal phrasing in brackets.
>
> [transcript]

That last instruction is the one that earns its keep. It stops the model quietly flattening the interesting parts, and it tells you where to be suspicious.

Two more that are worth having:

> Translate this transcript into English, then list any place where the original transcription looks garbled or where you had to guess at the meaning.

> Translate this into English and keep every number, product name, brand and measurement exactly as it appeared in the original.

The trade-off is real, though: an AI tool is more likely than DeepL to produce a fluent, confident sentence for something it didn't actually understand. Fluent and wrong is harder to spot than clumsy and wrong. If the stakes are high — medical, legal, financial — translate it twice with different tools and compare where they disagree.

The general workflow for feeding transcripts to models is covered in [using a TikTok transcript with ChatGPT](/tiktok-transcript-for-chatgpt); translation is one of the things it's genuinely reliable at.

## Where errors come from

Translation inherits every mistake the transcription made. If a brand name came through phonetically, the translator will faithfully translate the wrong word. Check the original transcript before you translate it, not after.

The predictable failure points:

- **Proper nouns** — names, brands and places are the most common transcription errors in any language, and translation compounds them.
- **Code-switching** — creators mixing English marketing terms into another language. Both stages handle this badly.
- **Numbers and units** — worth checking individually every time. A misheard price or dosage survives translation intact.
- **Sped-up delivery** — fast or artificially accelerated audio degrades transcription accuracy noticeably, and everything downstream with it.

Accuracy on clear speech sits in the mid-to-high 90s. Loud background music, fast delivery and heavy slang push it down. ## When you need a video tool instead

Being clear about the limits: TranscribeTok produces **text**. It doesn't translate, it doesn't dub, and it doesn't render subtitles onto video. If your goal is a finished translated video rather than translated words, a transcript tool is the wrong shape of tool and you should use one of these instead.

| What you need | Right tool | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Translated text you can read, quote or edit | TranscribeTok + DeepL or an AI tool | Free, two videos a day, no signup |
| Translated transcript in one step | WayinVideo, Dokitscript | Fewer steps; signup required |
| Translated subtitles rendered onto the video | VEED, VMEG | Video editors, not transcript tools |
| Dubbed audio in another language | VMEG, Vozo | Voice cloning and lip sync; paid |
| Live translation of a TikTok LIVE | JotMe | Real-time, different job entirely |

If all you want is to understand and quote what was said, the two-step method is faster and free. If you're republishing the video for another market, use a video translation tool — that's what they're built for and we're not competing on it.

**Can TikTok translate spoken audio?**
No. See Translation covers written captions and text stickers only.

**How do I translate a TikTok into English free?**
Transcribe the audio to text, then translate the text in DeepL, Google Translate or an AI tool.

No. It returns the transcript in the language spoken. Translation is a separate step.

**AI tool or Google Translate?**
An AI tool, usually — it handles slang and irony better if you give it context. But it's likelier to sound confident about something it got wrong.

Not from a transcript tool. Use a video translation tool like VEED or VMEG.
