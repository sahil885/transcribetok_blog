Converting a TikTok to text sounds like it should be a one-tap feature. It is not — TikTok has no export button anywhere in the app, and the captions it displays on screen cannot be selected or copied.

The workaround is straightforward: run the video's audio through a transcription tool and get plain text back. This guide covers how to do it, what the output looks like, and the cases where it will not give you what you expect.

## What "TikTok to text" actually captures

This is the part most guides skip, and it causes the most confusion.

Conversion works from the **audio track**. It captures what people say. It does not capture:

- **Text overlays** the creator typed onto the video. Those are rendered graphics, not text data.
- **Song lyrics** playing under the voice, at least not reliably. Music is noise to a speech model.
- **On-screen captions as a data source.** The tool does not read TikTok's caption track; it re-transcribes the audio, which is why it still works on videos where captions were never enabled.

For talking-head content — advice, storytelling, tutorials, reviews, most of what people want transcripts of — this is exactly right. For a silent video whose entire message is typed on screen, there is nothing to convert.

## Accuracy and the three things that hurt it

Expect mid-to-high 90s accuracy for clear speech. What degrades it, in rough order of impact:

1. **Loud background music.** Trending sounds mixed high over the voice.
2. **Multiple speakers talking over each other.** Duets and stitches are the usual culprits.
3. **Heavy slang, brand names and code-switching.** Platform-native vocabulary is where models miss most often.

None of this makes the output unusable — it is more than good enough to search, summarize and skim. Just read a quote back against the video before you publish it. Same rule as any automatic transcription tool.
