Most creators treat a TikTok as a finished thing. Post it, watch the numbers, move on to the next one.

That is a waste of the most expensive part. Writing a 45-second script means deciding what to say, choosing an angle, sequencing an argument and finding an opening that holds attention. All of that survives outside TikTok. What doesn't survive is the format.

The transcript is the bridge.

## Why the transcript is a better starting point than a blank page

A staring-at-nothing first draft has to solve two problems at once: what to say, and how to say it well. Working from a transcript, the first problem is already solved. You are only reformatting.

There is also a quality argument that gets overlooked. A TikTok script is written under real constraint — you had two seconds to earn attention and forty to make a point. That discipline produces tighter thinking than most people manage writing a blog post with no constraint at all. Repurposing that upward tends to work better than repurposing a blog post downward.

## The workflow

**Step 1: Transcribe.** Paste the TikTok link into [TranscribeTok](https://transcribetok.com) and get the script as text. Two free every day.

**Step 2: Strip the platform layer.** Delete "follow for more", "link in bio", "wait for it", and the verbal filler that exists to hold attention rather than carry meaning. Usually 20–30% of the words. What remains is the argument.

**Step 3: Reshape for the destination.** Each format wants something different:

| Format | What to change |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | Longer opening line, no slang, add a concrete takeaway |
| Newsletter section | Add the context the video assumed, keep the voice |
| X thread | Split at the natural beats — TikTok scripts usually have 3–5 |
| Blog section | Add depth the 45-second limit forced you to cut |
| YouTube Short / Reel | Mostly reusable; adjust the hook for platform norms |

**Step 4: Archive it.** Every transcript goes in one searchable place. Six months in, your back catalogue becomes an idea bank you can search instead of a folder you scroll.

## Do your back catalogue first

The instinct is to start repurposing new videos going forward. Start backwards instead.

Your existing posts are content you have already paid for, and the ones that performed well are proven — you know the hook worked, because the numbers said so. Almost nobody in your audience saw all of it, and anyone who found you in the last three months saw none of it.

Be selective about it. Sort your posts by performance, take the top twenty, and transcribe those over a couple of sittings. Twenty proven scripts are worth far more than a hundred transcribed indiscriminately — and that is roughly a quarter of content that is already mostly written.

## Where AI fits, and where it doesn't

Pasting a transcript into ChatGPT and asking for a LinkedIn post produces something publishable and slightly generic. Used well, though, AI removes the tedious parts:

- **Reformatting** — restructuring the same argument for a different platform's conventions
- **Expanding** — turning a compressed point into a paragraph with the detail the video had to cut
- **Extracting** — pulling every claim, tip or step out of a script into a clean list
- **Pattern-finding** — spotting which hooks recur across your own transcripts

What it should not do is replace your voice. The most useful prompt shape is "restructure this for X, keep my wording wherever possible" rather than "write me a post about this." [The full prompt set is here](/tiktok-transcript-for-chatgpt).

## Mining other people's transcripts (properly)

Transcripts are equally useful pointed outward. Reading fifty videos from creators one tier above you shows the formulas — and they are formulas, not inspiration. Most successful accounts rotate four or five hook templates.

The line is straightforward: **analyse structure, write your own words**. Noticing that a creator opens 60% of their videos with a specific-number claim is research. Copying their script is theft, and obvious to anyone following both of you.

## A realistic weekly rhythm

Something like this fits in about an hour:

1. **Monday:** transcribe last week's two best-performing posts
2. **Monday:** pick the two that performed best
3. **Tuesday:** turn one into a LinkedIn post and a newsletter section
4. **Thursday:** turn the other into an X thread and a script for another platform
5. **Ongoing:** everything lands in the archive, searchable later

Two videos become six pieces of content, and none of them started from a blank page.

Four or five comfortably.
