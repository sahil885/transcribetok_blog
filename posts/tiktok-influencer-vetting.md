Most influencer vetting looks at numbers: follower growth, median views, engagement rate, audience location. Those catch fake audiences. They do not catch the creator who mentioned your client's biggest competitor in last month's video, or who has never once said "ad" out loud in a paid post.

That risk lives in what the creator *says*, and TikTok gives you no way to search it. This guide shows agencies how to add a transcript check to TikTok influencer vetting, and what it costs.

## Why stats-only vetting misses the biggest risks

**Metrics tell you whether a creator's audience is real; they don't tell you what the creator has said to it.** Vetting has two halves: audience and performance, then content (brand safety, values, past partnerships and disclosure habits).

Agency checklists are specific about the content half. One widely shared approach reviews a creator's last 15 to 30 videos for fit and their last 10 sponsored posts for disclosure. Another, from influencer agency inBeat, reviews the last 5 to 10 sponsored posts, treats three recent undisclosed ones as a pattern, and flags any recent competitor partnership as a condition to check before signing.

Every one of those checks depends on what was spoken. TikTok shows captions during playback but offers no copy, export or download of them, so the content half of vetting means watching 20 or 30 videos per creator, and it is the half that gets skipped when the shortlist is long.

## What to search for in a creator's transcripts

**Four searches find most vetting problems: spoken disclosures, competitor mentions, risky claims and values conflicts.**

**Spoken disclosures.** Search past sponsored videos for "ad", "sponsored", "partner", "gifted" and "#ad" read aloud. TikTok requires creators to switch on its commercial content disclosure setting for posts that promote a brand, which adds a "Paid partnership" or "Commercial content" label, and TikTok says undisclosed commercial content may not be eligible for the For You feed. The US Federal Trade Commission goes further: its guide for influencers says a video disclosure should be in the video, not just the description, and that viewers are more likely to notice disclosures made in both audio and video. It also counts free or discounted products as a connection that must be disclosed. A creator whose paid posts never say it out loud is a contract-clause conversation before signing.

**Competitor mentions.** Search every transcript for the client's competitors by brand and product name. Agency checklists treat a recent competitor partnership as a conditional flag that needs an exclusivity check, and creators often recommend products unprompted in videos that carry no partnership label at all.

**Risky claims.** Search for numbers, percentages, "cure", "guaranteed", "lose", "earn" and dosage words. Health, finance and supplements clients care most, because a creator who makes unsupported results claims in organic videos will make them in yours.

**Values conflicts.** Search for topics the client has ruled out, plus profanity if the brand is family-facing. A tonal mismatch can be briefed around; a values conflict cannot.

## What a transcript can't tell you

**A transcript captures speech and nothing else, so three vetting checks still need the video itself.**

- **On-screen text and visuals.** Text overlays, gestures, products shown but not named, and the setting of the video are not in the audio. A video carried by typed captions over music returns little or no transcript; TranscribeTok charges no credit when a video has no spoken audio.
- **Deleted and private videos.** Only public videos can be transcribed. A recently scrubbed feed is a data point in itself.
- **The audience.** Comments, follower quality and engagement still need your metrics tool or a manual comment review.

Transcription is less accurate over loud music, sped-up audio or heavy slang, so confirm any line that decides a "drop" by watching that video.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is influencer vetting?**
Influencer vetting is the check a brand or agency runs on a creator before paying them for a partnership. It usually covers audience quality, performance, brand safety, past sponsorships and disclosure habits, and ends in a documented decision to proceed, proceed with conditions, or drop the creator.

**How many TikTok videos should you review when vetting a creator?**
Agency checklists commonly review a creator's last 15 to 30 videos for content and brand fit, plus their last 5 to 10 sponsored posts for disclosure. Twenty recent videos per creator is a practical middle figure, and large creators or regulated categories such as health and finance justify going further back.

**How do you check if a TikTok creator discloses sponsorships?**
Check two things on each past sponsored video: whether TikTok's Paid partnership or Commercial content label is shown, and whether the creator says the relationship out loud. The FTC says a video disclosure should be in the video itself, not just the description, and that viewers are more likely to notice disclosures made in both audio and video. A transcript shows the spoken part in seconds.
