Every TikTok audit template asks the same questions: which videos performed, which flopped, what posting time works. TikTok Studio answers those with numbers. None of them answers the question your manager actually asks next: *what did we say in the videos that worked?*

That answer is in the audio, and TikTok gives you no way to export it. This guide shows social media managers how to add transcripts to a TikTok content audit, what to look for once you have them, and exactly what it costs to transcribe 100 videos.

## What a TikTok audit misses without transcripts

**A standard TikTok audit measures performance, not message.** Sprout Social's audit method reviews profile setup, followers, content and competitors, and its author compared the top 50 and bottom 50 videos of a year, 100 in total. That comparison covers format, topic, audio and posting frequency. It does not cover the words spoken in each video.

TikTok Studio has the same blind spot. Its content analytics report views, average watch time, watched-full-video rate and where views came from, and desktop offers a CSV export of those numbers. There is no transcript, no script and no searchable text of what was said. TikTok's auto-captions play on screen but cannot be copied or downloaded anywhere in the app.

So the most actionable part of short-form video, the script, is the one part a social media manager cannot audit from the platform.

## The audit, step by step

**The workflow is: pull the numbers, transcribe the videos, put both in one sheet, then compare the top group with the bottom group.** Here it is in order.

1. **Pull the performance data.** In TikTok Studio, list the top 50 and bottom 50 videos for your audit period by views, along with average watch time and watched-full-video rate. A smaller account can use 20 and 20; keep the two groups the same size.
2. **Transcribe each video.** Paste each public video link into [TranscribeTok](https://transcribetok.com). It transcribes the audio track itself, so it works on videos that never had captions turned on. It processes **one link at a time**: 100 videos is 100 pastes. There is no bulk import.
3. **Build one sheet.** One row per video: link, views, average watch time, the hook (first two sentences of the transcript), the spoken call to action, any spoken claims, and the full transcript in the last column. Copy the transcript straight in, or download TXT, DOC or PDF if you are archiving files for a client.
4. **Compare the groups.** Read the top 50 hooks together, then the bottom 50. Do the same for calls to action. Write down only differences you can quote.
5. **Run the compliance and brand pass.** Search the transcript column for partner names, product claims and banned phrases (next section).

On a credit pack, every transcript is also saved to your TranscribeTok library, so next quarter's audit starts with this quarter's scripts already filed.

## What to look for in the transcripts

**Four comparisons produce most of the findings: hooks, calls to action, brand voice, and spoken claims and disclosures.**

**Hooks.** The first fifteen to twenty-five words of a transcript are the spoken hook. Put the top-group hooks next to the bottom-group hooks and count patterns: questions, numbers, direct "you", a contradiction, a product name in the first sentence. A finding like "31 of our top 50 open with a number; 6 of our bottom 50 do" is something a team can act on. "Our best videos feel more authentic" is not.

**Calls to action.** List every spoken CTA. Many brand accounts discover their CTAs are inconsistent ("link in bio", "check our site", "comment below") or missing from half the videos.

**Brand voice.** Search the transcript column for your category jargon and for the phrases your audience uses instead. If top-performing videos say "gets the smell out" and the brand guidelines say "odour elimination", that is a guideline worth rewriting.

**Spoken claims and disclosures.** Search for prices, percentages, guarantees and health or results claims, then check each against what legal approved. For paid creator content, check the disclosure too. The US Federal Trade Commission says a video endorsement disclosure should be in the video, not just the description, and that viewers are more likely to notice disclosures made in both audio and video. A transcript shows instantly which partnership videos never say it out loud.

## Where transcripts fall short in an audit

**A transcript captures the spoken audio and nothing else.** Three things in a TikTok audit still need eyes on the video.

- **On-screen text.** Text overlays are graphics, not data. A video that carries its message in typed captions over music returns an empty or near-empty transcript. TranscribeTok charges no credit when a video has no spoken audio, but you still have to watch it.
- **Visual hooks and edit pace.** A transcript cannot tell you that the first frame was a product close-up or that cuts came every second.
- **Private and deleted videos.** Only public videos can be transcribed. Drafts, friends-only posts and deleted videos are out of reach for every tool.

## Turning the audit into a report

**End the audit with three to five findings, each backed by a quoted line and a number.** For example: "Top-group videos state the price in the first ten seconds (18 of 50); bottom-group videos rarely do (4 of 50)." Pair each finding with a test for next month.

If you want an AI assistant to help find patterns, paste the hook column into it rather than the links. AI tools cannot watch a TikTok; they can read transcripts. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is a TikTok content audit?**
A TikTok content audit is a structured review of a brand's published TikTok videos to find out what separates the best performers from the worst. Most audits compare performance metrics such as views and watch time; adding transcripts lets you compare what was actually said, including hooks, claims and calls to action.

**How many videos should a TikTok audit include?**
A common approach is to review your top 50 and bottom 50 videos for the period, 100 in total. Smaller accounts can audit fewer, but keep an even mix of high and low performers, because the comparison between the two groups is where the findings come from.

**How much does it cost to transcribe 100 TikTok videos for an audit?**

**Does TikTok analytics show what was said in each video?**
No. TikTok Studio analytics reports performance numbers such as views, average watch time, watched-full-video rate and traffic sources, but it has no transcript, script or spoken-word data. TikTok's auto-captions can be watched but cannot be copied, exported or downloaded, so the spoken content has to be transcribed separately.

Not with TranscribeTok, which processes one public video link at a time and has no bulk import. Bulk tools such as TokScript, GetTranscribe and the Apify TikTok transcript actors can pull many videos at once and suit agencies auditing several full profiles.
