Most TikTok competitor research is done by scrolling. You watch fifteen videos, form an impression, and write a slide that says the competitor is "leaning into authenticity". Nobody can act on that.

The fix is to turn the videos into text. A transcript of each video gives you something you can search, quote, compare and paste into an AI tool, and the first two sentences of every transcript become a TikTok hooks swipe file built from what actually ran in your category.

## Why marketers transcribe TikToks instead of watching them

A transcript turns a competitor's video library into a searchable text corpus. That enables four things watching cannot: searching dozens of videos for one phrase, quoting an exact claim in a strategy document, reading scripts side by side, and pasting a whole set into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini in one prompt.

The second effect matters more. Reading a script strips out the lighting, the editing pace, the creator's charisma and the trending sound. What is left is the argument and its order, which is the part you can reproduce without their face or their budget.

## What to extract from each transcript

Five things are worth pulling out of every transcript. Hooks are the main prize, but the other four are what turn a swipe file into a positioning document.

**Hooks.** A TikTok hook occupies roughly the first three to six seconds, which is the first fifteen to twenty-five words of a transcript. Copy the opening two sentences of every video into one file. Patterns show up within an afternoon: how many open on a number, how many on a contradiction, how many on "you" addressed directly.

**Claims.** Every specific number a competitor says out loud — a price, a percentage, a timeframe, a guarantee — is a claim you can log, check and position against. Claims are hard to catch while watching and trivial to find in text.

**Pre-empted objections.** Good short-form scripts answer the viewer's doubt before it forms: "I know this sounds expensive, but…". Those sentences tell you what a competitor believes its market resists. They are the most useful lines in the transcript and the easiest to miss on a first watch.

**Vocabulary.** Note every phrase used in place of your category's jargon. If their audience says "gets rid of the smell" and your site says "odour neutralisation technology", the transcript has written your next landing page.

**Structure.** With ten transcripts in one document, the shared skeleton becomes visible: hook, credential, problem, mechanism, proof, call to action, or whatever your category's pattern turns out to be.

## The workflow, start to finish

Transcribe ten to thirty videos per competitor, not a hundred. Ten read closely beat a hundred skimmed, and scripts in one category converge fast.

1. **Pick the videos by performance.** Sort by views and engagement over the last ninety days. A swipe file of videos that were merely visible to you teaches the wrong lessons.
2. **Transcribe them one at a time.** Paste each public link into [TranscribeTok](https://transcribetok.com). It processes one video per paste, so thirty videos is thirty pastes, roughly ten seconds each. There is no batch mode and we are not going to pretend otherwise.
3. **Copy or download each transcript.** Plain TXT is the right format here because you will concatenate everything into one document. [Choosing between TXT, DOC and PDF](/download-tiktok-transcript) covers the differences.
4. **Split the document twice.** One file for hooks (the first two sentences of each), one for claims and objections.
5. **Ask a structural question.** Paste the full set into an AI tool. "Summarise these ten videos" wastes the exercise; "What structure do these ten scripts share, and which two break it?" does not. ## Where a transcript is the wrong tool

A transcript reads the audio track and nothing else. That answers "what did they say, and in what order", and it fails on three common marketing questions.

| Research question | Does a transcript answer it? | What you need instead |
|---|---|---|
| What did they say, and in what order? | Yes | A transcript |
| What claims and numbers did they state? | Yes | A transcript |
| What does the on-screen text say? | **No** | Watch the video or take a screenshot |
| What was the visual hook or edit pace? | **No** | Watch the first five seconds |
| How did the video perform? | **No** | Platform analytics or a social listening tool |

The on-screen text limit catches marketers out most. Many TikTok ads carry the message in typed overlays over music, with no voiceover at all. Those videos return an empty or near-empty transcript. That is not a tool failure; there is no speech to transcribe. TranscribeTok charges no credit when a video has no spoken audio, but you still have to watch it.

The performance limit matters as much. A transcript tells you what was said, never how well it did. Pair every hook with its view and engagement numbers before you decide the pattern works.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a TikTok hooks swipe file?**
A document of the opening lines from high-performing TikTok videos, collected so you can study and adapt the patterns. Ten to thirty per competitor or per theme. Fewer than ten and you mistake noise for a pattern; beyond thirty the returns drop because short-form scripts in one category converge quickly.

**Does a TikTok transcript include the on-screen text hook?**
